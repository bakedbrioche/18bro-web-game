# Product overview

## Origin

18Bro is a black cat from Hong Kong who became a viral meme after stealing chips from a local 7-Eleven. The incident established the character's mischievous snack-heist personality. The company behind the character is developing 18Bro into a broader mascot IP with merchandise and content, including an official Hong Kong 7-Eleven merchandise release.

This repository creates a community web game around that story for a related crypto project. It is not an official, licensed, or partnered production. The intended opportunity is to meet the current cultural moment with a polished, globally accessible game that people can open from a link and understand immediately.

The original public URL was `18bro.fun`, but its previous server is unavailable. Treat the URL as **TBU — to be updated** until the operator confirms a live destination. The approved project tag is **Run for your chips.**

## Product goal

Build a charming, highly shareable browser arcade game that makes 18Bro recognizable, preserves the humor of the original event, and gives the related crypto community a strong promotional experience. The immediate goal is to finish the major visual touches within 24 hours so the game can be release-ready around the official merchandise launch.

The game should:

- work instantly on a dedicated URL without account creation, wallet connection, or installation;
- feel good on desktop and mobile;
- make the hero cat appealing in menus, animation, and the normal rear gameplay view;
- use Hong Kong and convenience-store details respectfully and legibly;
- encourage short repeat runs and sharing;
- remain stable enough for global traffic as a static site.

The initial launch should support English, Traditional Chinese, and Cantonese. Yandex Metrica and Meta Pixel must be supported through a disabled-by-default, consent-aware analytics boundary once measurement IDs and policy copy are supplied.

## Player experience

The player steals chips from a Hong Kong convenience store, escapes into a continuous street, dodges traffic and barriers, collects chips, and outruns two police officers. The tone is playful petty chaos rather than violence.

## Current product

- One continuous WebGL chase through market, commercial, intersection, and neon districts.
- Keyboard, touch, pause, mute, reduced-motion, and fullscreen controls.
- Local records, missions, chip balance, fur colors, and hats stored in browser `localStorage`.
- Powerups: magnet, Moon Boots, Speed Burst, electric scooter, shield, double chips, and jetpack, each with an authored 3D pickup, aura and matching icon. Magnet and boots remain visible on the cat; Moon Boots own high jump while Speed Burst owns acceleration.
- No account, backend, API key, active analytics, or shared leaderboard.

## Delivery surfaces

- Modular game: `dist/index.html`, served locally with `npm start` and hosted from `dist/`.
- Review tools: `dist/preview.html` and the in-game developer panel.
- Standalone release: generated by `scripts/export-game.mjs`; local output goes to `release/`.

## Stack

- Static HTML/CSS and browser-native ES modules.
- Vendored Three.js/WebGL renderer; no runtime CDN.
- Procedural Three.js environment, vehicles and Original character family; Blender-authored GLB cat, human and power-up assets plus generated icons for Refreshed.
- Node's built-in test runner for deterministic simulation/model regressions.
- Dependency-free Node static server (`server.mjs`) for local development.
- Browser `localStorage` for device-local progress and settings.
- Static hosting from `dist/` through `.openai/hosting.json`.
- Standalone one-file exporter for offline/shareable builds.

There is currently no backend, account system, wallet connection, token integration, shared leaderboard, telemetry, or asset-build dependency.

Localization and analytics boundaries are specified in `../architecture/localization-analytics.md`. Analytics vendor IDs remain TBU and must never be committed as secrets; public measurement IDs belong in explicit release configuration.

## Product standard

The visual target is a readable, charming, PS1-inspired game rather than indiscriminate low resolution. The 18Bro silhouette, expressive face, Hong Kong setting, obstacle readability, responsive controls, and stable performance take priority over decorative detail that disappears during play.

Players can switch between Original and Refreshed visual families. Refreshed loads the authored cat, human and power-up templates atomically and stays consistent through every complete hero clip, cosmetic, officer, cashier, rider, nearby crowd member and pickup. Responsive roll/jump chaining and scooter-specific mount/jump/dodge poses are part of the product feel. Clear Retro, Classic Pixel and Coarser picture modes remain independent from the model family. Arcade and 18Bro Sticker typography are also independent saved preferences.
